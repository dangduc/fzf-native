# fzf-native benchmark report — 2026-09-07

Lower times are better unless a table states otherwise. All live runs used one Apple M3 MacBook Air and ran serially.

## Main results

- Eight workers reduced the interactive prefix-session median from 485.84 ms to 122.30 ms. This result is a 3.97x speedup.
- Cached backspace results returned in 4.21–13.85 µs. These times are close to the polling floor of the driver.
- On the 95,655-item `emacs` workload, fzf-native took 6.918 ms for scores and 8.853 ms with positions.
- On the harder `e-macs` workload, fzf-native took 10.036 ms for scores and 23.667 ms with positions.
- Frizbee was fastest in all three real data-set groups.
- fzf-native beat Nucleo on Arabic by 1.27x and Korean by 1.51x. Nucleo beat fzf-native on Chromium by 1.72x.
- In Emacs, the fastest fzf-native configuration had a 0.2198 s median for 20 calls. `basic` had a 0.1444 s median.

The clearest optimization target is position retrieval. It added 28.0% on `emacs` and 135.8% on `e-macs` in fuzzy-benches.

## Interactive session benchmark

The Skim fork supplied the harness. The timed matcher was fzf-native, not Skim.

The driver loaded 1,000,000 generated candidates. Each round emitted at most 10,000 results.

Each configuration used two warmups and 10 measured sessions.

The harness validated each result against an untimed full scan. All 220 JSONL records with a `verified` field contain `true`.

| `m` growth | 93.30 ms | 34.32 ms | 2.72x |

The fixed style order and five processes limit inference from close rankings. Process noise changed the fastest fzf-native row in two runs.

The harness disabled the fussy cache and used case-insensitive matching. The exact mean candidate length is 37.579 characters, and the median is 32.

The harness truncates the mean to 37 and labels it as a median. Both operations make the displayed label incorrect.

Emacs warned that it used older byte-compiled files for fussy, flx, and orderless. The same isolated package directory supplied all five processes.

## Interpretation

The interactive session work scales well to eight workers. Cached backspace results avoid a full rescan.

The batch holdouts show a mixed result. fzf-native performs well on Unicode data but loses on Chromium and to the fastest specialized matcher.

The strongest direct target is the position path. The `e-macs` position cost is too large to explain as wrapper overhead.

The Emacs macrobenchmark gives useful end-to-end data. Its process spread and fixed order make it unsuitable for small regression claims.

The host was not in a dedicated benchmark mode. macOS background work and heterogeneous core placement can affect the measurements.
